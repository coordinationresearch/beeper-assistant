# Playbook

Moves for handling people over text, one section per situation, distilled from books on conversation, friendship, and networking. Each section ends with the books it draws on. Read the section for the situation in front of you and use what fits this Person and this Chat. The messages, the Person's Notes, and how the Owner writes there outrank any move here, and the Owner's Lessons outrank the move they disagree with. Never put a book's name or a move's name in a message.

## Reading how they feel

When: replies get shorter or slower than this person's usual, emoji and punctuation drop away, a bare "k", "fine", or "haha" arrives, the tone turns formal, or the Owner asks "is she mad at me?"

- Read each text against this person's own history. Look for a change from their usual length, reply gap, punctuation, and emoji, or words that clash with those cues, like "all good!" after two days of silence.
- Keep the fact apart from the story. "Replied 'ok' after six hours" is a fact and "she's annoyed" is one story, so give the Owner a second plausible reading and don't comb old messages for proof.
- Treat going quiet and lashing out as the same signal. After dodged questions, "lol" deflections, sarcasm, or "you always", step off the topic and restate good intent before going back to it.
- Name the feeling in one tentative clause, then move on. "Sounds like this week has been a lot" beats a paragraph of validation.
- Echo instead of interrogating. Send their key word or two back as a question ("the whole weekend?"), and match their length and energy, so a three-word text gets a short reply.
- Look for a second topic inside a reply. If a question about Friday gets back "must be nice to have free time", answer Friday in one line and name the other thing separately ("that sounded like it's about more than Friday").
- Notice when a topic has run out. Longer gaps, "haha" with nothing after it, and repeats mean change the subject or let the thread rest.

Avoid:
- Treating one curt text as a verdict and drafting an apology for an offense nobody named.
- Asking "why" ("why are you being short?"), which reads as an accusation and makes people dig in. Ask with "what" or "how".
- Counting a flat "sure" or "sounds good" after a long pause as agreement. Confirm the specifics ("great, 7 at Rosa's?") before relying on it.

Example: Dana, usually chatty, answers "ok" six hours later. Instead of "Are you mad at me? You seem off.", send "Seems like today got away from you. Thursday can wait."

Books: Goleman, *Emotional Intelligence*; Voss, *Never Split the Difference*; Grenny, *Crucial Conversations*; Stone, *Thanks for the Feedback*; Brooks, *Talk*; Grant, *Think Again*.

## Their good news

When: their message announces a new job, promotion, engagement, launch, acceptance or win, often with caps, "!!" or "guess what", or the Owner asks to congratulate someone.

- Match the register of their message before adding warmth. Caps and three exclamation marks get energy back, while a quiet one-line mention gets a short, warm reply instead of a row of emoji.
- Name the exact win and the work that got them there. "Four rounds and a take-home, and you got it" beats "so smart" or "so deserved".
- Ask one question that lets them tell it again, such as how they found out or which part they are proudest of. Keep the answer for later texts.
- Keep the spotlight on them. Leave the Owner's own similar win or "same thing happened to me" out of this reply.
- If the Owner heard the news from someone else, say who and pass on anything kind that person said ("Sam told me, he was beaming"). Never invent it.
- Send the warm line freely, and save "you're the most ___ person I know" for a real milestone.
- Offer a specific way to celebrate with a day attached, or a small treat, instead of "we should celebrate!"

Avoid:
- A bare "Congrats!!" or "So deserved 🎉" with no detail, which reads as a cliché sent to everyone.
- Comparing or self-deprecating ("wish my year looked like this lol").
- Tacking a caveat onto the congratulations ("just don't let them overwork you").
- Skipping a congratulations because it is days late. Send it with a few words of apology at most.

Example: Maya writes "got the design lead job!!" A weak reply is "Congrats!! So deserved." A better one is "MAYA. Four rounds and a take-home, and you got it. How did they tell you?"

Books: Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Haidt, *The Happiness Hypothesis*; Dweck, *Mindset*; Brooks, *Talk*; Franco, *Platonic*; Barker, *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*; Carnegie, *How to Win Friends and Influence People*.

## Hard news

When: they mention bad news, such as a death, diagnosis, hospital stay, layoff, breakup, or failed plan ("got laid off today"), or the Owner asks what to say to someone going through one.

- Open by naming what happened, in their words. Hold advice and silver linings for later.
- Keep the reply about as long as their message.
- Ask one open follow-up, then shift. Move toward perspective or practical help instead of going over the feelings again.
- Ask before offering a different view, then widen it. Check first ("want my take, or just need to vent?"), then zoom out in time or normalize it ("this round cut a lot of strong people").
- Offer only what the Owner named, and make it specific and reply-free. "Leaving soup on your porch Thursday, no need to answer" beats "let me know if you need anything". When the Owner named nothing, the reply is warmth alone: invent no offer of your own, even in a draft.
- Check in later without being asked. A short "thinking of you today" a week on, or on the hard date.
- After a setback like a layoff, point hope at the person, not the event. "You'll land somewhere good" helps where "everything happens for a reason" doesn't.

Avoid:
- Job-search tips, "at least" lines, or any fix in the first reply.
- Joining the outrage at the ex or the boss.
- Steering around the subject in later chats for fear of upsetting them.

Example: Sam texts "got laid off today", and the Owner wants to drop off dinner Thursday. Instead of "Oh no! Everything happens for a reason. Want me to look at your resume?", send "Ugh, Sam, I'm so sorry. That's a gut punch. Dropping off dinner Thursday, no need to reply."

Books: Voss, *Never Split the Difference*; Goleman, *Emotional Intelligence*; Brooks, *Talk*; Kross, *Chatter*; Franco, *Platonic*.

## Keeping a conversation alive

When: a thread is going back and forth and the Owner asks what to say next, the other person's replies are getting shorter, slower or only "haha", or an older thread is worth picking back up.

- Make the next text a follow-up on their last message, ideally on its oddest detail (a place, a name, a strange word), in their own words. If they said "gig", don't write "job".
- Send one question per text and pair it with something of the Owner's own, an opinion or a small fact.
- Call back to earlier threads. Ask how the thing they mentioned last time turned out, and reuse a running joke from earlier in the Chat.
- Find the fun in what is already there instead of a prepared line, for example by treating a small detail from their message as a huge deal. If it flops, label it in three words and move on.
- Switch topics when a thread wears out: slower replies, "haha" or "lol" with nothing else, and the same point coming back.
- Let a thread end on a high instead of fading into "ok" or a last logistics check. Once the plan is set or the joke has landed, send one warm or funny closing line and don't add a new question.

Avoid:
- A chain of questions with nothing of the Owner's in between, which turns a chat into an interview.
- Teasing at their expense, even mild teasing, since a text carries no tone of voice to soften it.
- Answering a two-line text with a paragraph that covers every point.

Example: Leo writes "took a pottery class, made a bowl". A weak reply is "Nice! What else have you been up to?" A better one is "You made a BOWL? I need a photo and a ruling on whether it holds soup."

Books: Brooks, *Talk*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Parker, *The Art of Gathering*.

## Going deeper with a friend

When: a friend's thread has been plans, links or "haha" for weeks, they mention something heavy in passing ("work's fine, dad's back in the hospital lol"), or the Owner asks how to reconnect or show they care.

- Answer the heavy aside before anything else in their message, and invite more with a short "wait, say more?" instead of a fix.
- Swap "how's the new job?" for a question that asks for a feeling or a story, such as the best and worst part so far, what they are most excited about lately, or what has changed for them.
- Go first with one small, true admission from the Owner, then see whether one comes back. If they answer with logistics, stay at that level.
- Ask the hard question once, plainly, and if the answer is short, take that as their level for now.
- Say the affection outright and tie it to a moment, like "saw a corgi in a raincoat and thought of you" or "honestly I miss you".
- When they are struggling, offer only what the Owner named, and make it specific, with a day ("I can bring dinner Wednesday"), instead of "let me know if you need anything". When the Owner named nothing, stay with the question and the warmth: invent no offer of your own, even in a draft.
- Propose a small ritual so contact stops depending on someone remembering, like a call every other Sunday or a standing walk.

Avoid:
- Advice or silver linings before the feeling ("at least they caught it early", "have you tried").
- A deep question dropped cold into a thread of logistics. Mention a shared memory before the big question.
- Topping a small disclosure with a bigger, heavier story.

Example: Jonah writes "work's fine, dad's back in the hospital lol". A weak reply is "Oh no, let me know if you need anything!" A better one is "Wait, he's back in? How's he doing, and how are you doing with it?"

Books: Coyle, *The Culture Code*; Stevenson and Greenberg, *The 12 Levers*; Franco, *Platonic*; Brooks, *Talk*; Barker, *Plays Well with Others*; Haidt, *The Happiness Hypothesis*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*.

## Someone new

When: a new contact or short Chat after a party, conference, app match or intro ("great meeting you!"), or the Owner asks how to follow up with someone they just met or get closer to an acquaintance.

- Follow up within a day or two and open with a hook from what they talked about, such as the ramen place they swore by or their half marathon.
- Say plainly that the Owner wants to see them again and attach a real plan, like "trivia Thursday, want in?" instead of "we should hang sometime".
- Prefer a repeat or group plan over one big one-on-one, such as a weekly run, a standing game night or a friend's party.
- Give first. Send the article, recommendation or intro they mentioned wanting.
- Ask for their advice or a recommendation in their area ("you've done Lisbon, what should I not miss?") instead of offering the Owner's.
- Turn a small moment from the meeting into a callback and use "we" early ("we still owe that karaoke machine an apology").
- Show a small weakness, not real trouble: a light self-deprecating line ("sorry I talked about spreadsheets for twenty minutes"), with the heavy stuff kept for close friends.

Avoid:
- Hedged openers like "not sure if you remember me" or "sorry to bother you".
- Asking for a favor, an intro or a meeting about the Owner's project in the first follow-up.
- Reading one slow reply as a no and dropping it. Send one more specific invite before letting it go.

Example: Dana writes "great meeting you last night!" A weak reply is "You too! We should grab coffee sometime." A better one is "You too! I tried your #2 ramen spot and you were right. Want to hit #1 Thursday?"

Books: Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*; Carnegie, *How to Win Friends and Influence People*; Franco, *Platonic*; Barker, *Plays Well with Others*; Haidt, *The Happiness Hypothesis*; Barker, *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Coyle, *The Culture Code*.

## Reconnecting after a long gap

When: the last message in the Chat is months or years old, or the Owner asks to reach out to, check in with, or reconnect with someone they've lost touch with.

- Open with the specific thing that brought them to mind, like a place, a song, or news about their company, then ask about their life now.
- Mention the gap in a few words or not at all, and write in the old thread's register, nicknames and running jokes included. Assume they still like the Owner.
- Read the end of the old thread before drafting and pick up its loose end, such as the move they were planning or the job they were interviewing for.
- Add one line of the Owner's own news, honest rather than a highlight reel, and let the other person set how deep it goes before sharing more.
- If the reconnect has an ask behind it, name it in the first or second message instead of staging a catch-up and pivoting three texts later.
- When there's a real occasion, like being in their city or going to the same event, propose a specific day instead of "we should catch up sometime".

Avoid:
- A greeting with nothing specific after it, like a bare "Hope you're well!", which reads as if it went to fifty people.
- A catch-up that turns into a pitch a few messages later.
- Formal phrasing like "I hope this message finds you well" with someone the Owner used to text in lowercase.

Example: To Jordan after two years of silence, instead of "Hey!! So sorry I've been MIA, life got crazy, hope you're doing well!", send "Walked past that terrible karaoke bar today and thought of you. Did you ever finish restoring the boat?"

Books: Licht, *On Brand*; Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*; Franco, *Platonic*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Cialdini, *Influence*; Jiang, *Rejection Proof*; Barker, *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*.

## Staying in touch without an agenda

When: no open thread, and the agent sees a birthday, new job, launch, move, or post from someone the Owner hasn't messaged in a while, or the Owner asks to keep in touch with someone.

- Send one specific thing with no ask attached, such as their news, a link that fits something they care about, or the moment they crossed the Owner's mind. "Saw this and thought of you" plus the link is a complete text.
- Give where it counts most. A doctor's name, a job lead, or a tip about their kid's school beats any generic gesture.
- Relay praise the Owner heard about them, like "Dana said your talk was the best of the day".
- When congratulating, name the quality behind the news, like "of course you got it, you out-prepare everyone", instead of a bare "congrats!".
- When they thank the Owner, answer with something like "anytime, you'd do the same for me" instead of "no worries".
- Match cadence to the tie, about monthly for the closest ring and two or three times a year for the next, and accept that many ties are best kept at a low dose. Keep a few ties outside the Owner's usual circle warm too.
- Every few pings, turn one into time together, like an invite to something already planned.

Avoid:
- Reaching out only when the Owner needs something, so every text becomes a sign an ask is coming.
- Keeping score, such as waiting because the other person owes a text.
- A birthday "HBD!" identical to everyone else's. Add one line only the Owner could write.

Example: Instead of "Hey stranger, how's everything?", send "Saw your team's launch made the front page. Of course it did, you rewrote that plan three times. Celebrating this weekend?"

Books: Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*; Franco, *Platonic*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Carnegie, *How to Win Friends and Influence People*; Cialdini, *Influence*; Putnam, *Bowling Alone*; Barker, *Plays Well with Others*; Barker, *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*.

## Asking for help

When: the Owner wants a favor, advice, a referral, or an intro for themselves, or says "help me ask X for...".

- Put the whole ask in the first text with the reason attached.
- Say why this person in particular, such as their experience, their role, or something they once said.
- Size the ask on purpose. With weak ties, keep it small. With closer ones you can aim high, with a smaller fallback in the same text.
- Name the imposition in a few words, like "I know this is out of the blue", and close with a real out, like "no stress if the timing's bad".
- For advice, ask what they did in a specific past situation, or for the one thing they'd change, never what they think of the Owner's idea.
- If they say no, thank them and retreat to something smaller, or ask who else they'd ask.

Avoid:
- "Hey, quick question" or "Got a sec?" with the ask held back.
- Asking right after doing them a favor, or pointing out what they owe.
- Going quiet after they help. Tell them what came of it.

Example: Instead of "Hey Ana, quick question when you have a sec?", send "Ana, I'm pricing my first freelance job and you're the only person I know who's done it. How did you set your rate with your first client? Totally fine if you're swamped."

Books: Jiang, *Rejection Proof*; Voss, *Never Split the Difference*; Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*; Barker, *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*; Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*; Cialdini, *Influence*; Grant, *Hidden Potential*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Licht, *On Brand*.

## Following up when they went quiet

When: the Owner's last message has sat unanswered for days or weeks, or the Owner asks "should I follow up?" or says "they never replied" or "she ghosted me".

- Assume the text got buried, not ignored, and write a follow-up that stands on its own without mentioning the silence.
- Bring something new each time, such as a detail, a link, or a narrower question, instead of "just following up".
- Make the second ask easier to answer, never vaguer. Keep the plan by name, such as the hike or the call, not a looser "catch up", and narrow it to something they can answer in a word: two times the Owner can do, a yes or no, or "which day this week?" when the old times have passed. Never "whenever works".
- For a stalled plan or decision, ask a question that makes "no" easy to say, like "Should I take this off your list?" People answer faster when saying no feels safe.
- If they already agreed to something, restate their own words plainly, like "you'd mentioned Thursday might work".
- Change the angle once or twice, then close the loop kindly and stop.
- When they finally reply, answer warmly as if no time passed.

Avoid:
- "Bumping this!" or "Did you see my last text?" with nothing new in it.
- Guilt or sarcasm, like "Guess you're busy" or a lone "?".
- Several texts in a row before they've answered.
- Telling the Owner the silence is a verdict on them.

Example: To Priya, a week after an unanswered dinner invite, instead of "Did you see my text?? 😅", send "Found a Thursday slot at that ramen place in case weekends are the problem. No pressure if this month's packed."

Books: Barker, *Barking Up the Wrong Tree*; Putnam, *Bowling Alone*; Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*; Jiang, *Rejection Proof*; Cialdini, *Influence*; Voss, *Never Split the Difference*; Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*; Carnegie, *How to Win Friends and Influence People*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*.

## Making an introduction

When: the Owner says "introduce", "connect them", or "you two should meet", or someone in a Chat asks "do you know anyone who...".

- When one side asked for the intro, ask the other side first in their own Chat, with a real way out like "totally fine to pass", since only the asker has said yes. Otherwise, between friends, go straight to the group, and ask first when one side is someone the Owner rarely talks to, or a work contact.
- Get a two-line reason from the person who wants the intro, covering what they're doing, what exactly they want, and why this person in particular, then paste it rather than paraphrasing.
- Give each person one or two lines that praise them specifically and end with a hook, such as "ask her about the food truck she ran in college".
- Name what the two share, such as the same school, the same city, or the same odd hobby.
- Suggest intros nobody asked for when one person's need matches another's skill, and favor pairs from different circles.

Avoid:
- The naked intro, "You two should meet!" with no reason and no hook.
- Adding someone the Owner rarely talks to, or a work contact, to a group with a stranger before asking them, or putting the person who didn't ask for the intro into a group before asking them.
- A full resume for each person.

Example: Instead of "Sam meet Lee, Lee meet Sam, you two should chat!", send "Sam, meet Lee, who ran ops for two bakeries and loves talking shop. Lee, Sam opens her first cafe in March, ask her about the 1950s oven she rescued."

Books: Cialdini, *Influence*; Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*; Lowndes, *How to Talk to Anyone*; Gray, *The 2-Hour Cocktail Party*; Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*; Gladwell, *The Tipping Point*; Putnam, *Bowling Alone*.

## Rallying a group

When: the Owner wants to plan a dinner, party, trip, or game night, or a group Chat keeps saying "we should do something" with no date.

- Open with what the gathering is for and a concrete plan, like "dinner Tuesday at 7 at Luca's to toast Dev's new job".
- Invite a core few one by one with a personal line about why them, then post to the group naming who's in.
- When a group thread stalls, text two or three people directly with a yes-or-no question.
- Ask "want the details?" before sending a link or a poll, and count only a typed "I'm in" as a yes.
- Give a few people a small job, like bringing dessert or picking the music.
- Decide on plus-ones before inviting. Keep a small dinner's list closed and a recurring group's open.
- Remind a week out with some excitement, three days out with who's coming and a hook for each, and the morning of with the address and timing. The next day, send thanks with the group photo and float the next date.

Avoid:
- An open "when works for everyone?" poll in a big group with no proposed date.
- An invite that opens with parking, address, and timing. Lead with why, and save logistics for the morning-of reminder.
- Fifteen people at a dinner meant for one conversation. Keep it to about six, twelve at most.

Example: Instead of "We should all get dinner soon! When's everyone free?", send "Booking a table for 6 at Luca's next Tuesday at 7 to toast Dev's new job. Sam and Ines are in. You in?"

Books: Parker, *The Art of Gathering*; Gray, *The 2-Hour Cocktail Party*; Vogl, *The Art of Community*; Cialdini, *Influence*; Richardson, *Get Together*; Ferrazzi, *Never Eat Alone*.

## Tension and repair

When: the Owner missed, forgot, or flaked on something, a comment landed badly, a thread went cold after a sharp exchange, the other person wrote "it's fine" or "whatever", or the Owner asks how to apologize or raise a grievance.

- Apologize in a line or two that names the impact, not the reason. "I left you waiting an hour, I'm sorry" works where "my phone died and traffic was insane" reads as an excuse.
- End on a concrete change instead of a request. Skip "can you forgive me?" and "are we ok?", which hand them the job of soothing the Owner.
- Own fault in proportion to it. Save "I'm sorry that left you stuck" for shared or unclear fault, and own the act plainly when the Owner caused it.
- Say the worst thing they're probably thinking before they do. After a long silence, "you probably figured I'd vanished, and that's fair".
- Raise a grievance in four beats. Say what the friendship means, state the facts, offer the Owner's reading as a guess, then ask, ending on something like "am I reading that wrong?"
- Match the level of the problem. If it's the third cancellation, name the pattern, and if the same fight keeps coming back, talk about the relationship under it.
- When the Owner is angry, draft the reply but suggest a pause before sending. Anger takes about 20 minutes to settle.

Avoid:
- "Sorry, but...", "sorry if you felt that way", or re-arguing who was right inside the repair.
- Taking all the blame to smooth things over when both sides contributed. Name the Owner's part and leave room for theirs.
- Waiting for it to blow over.

Example: Priya's birthday passed without a text. Instead of "So sorry!! Work has been insane and my phone died, hope you're not mad?", send "I missed your birthday and I'm sorry. It's in my calendar now. Lunch on me next week?"

Books: Goldsmith, *What Got You Here Won't Get You There*; Stone, *Thanks for the Feedback*; Brooks, *Talk*; Fisher, *Getting to Yes*; Voss, *Never Split the Difference*; Franco, *Platonic*; Grenny, *Crucial Conversations*; Goleman, *Emotional Intelligence*.

## Saying no or hard feedback

When: the Owner asks to decline an invite, favor, intro, or job offer, to push back on a plan, or to tell a friend something unwelcome, or a draft of theirs hedges until the point is gone.

- Put the no in the first line with one real reason. Name a plain priority ("I'm keeping weekends free this month") rather than a vague conflict.
- Turn down the request, and offer something else only when the Owner said they can give it. Work out what they're after, such as hands on moving day, and offer exactly the part the Owner named ("can't do Saturday, can bring boxes Friday night"). When the Owner named nothing, send the no with their reason as they gave it and no substitute, not even "let me know if I can help".
- Be warm and firm in the same message, joined by "and". Thank them for asking, say no once and clearly, and affirm the friendship, never "I'd love to, but".
- Critique the act, not the person, with situation, behavior, and impact. Write "at dinner, the jokes about her job made her go quiet" instead of "you're so insensitive".
- Decide whether unasked-for criticism goes in at all. Ask whether it is true, worth it, and will help, and say it plainly if it passes.
- On someone's own life choices, ask before advising, then ask more than tell. Open with "want my honest take?", then use open questions and reflect their words back.
- Say how big it is and leave room to push back. Size it ("small thing, for next time") and end on an opening like "tell me if I'm off".

Avoid:
- Hedging until the point is gone ("maybe, I'll see, not sure yet") when the Owner means no.
- Splitting the difference with a half-yes the Owner doesn't want, like "maybe I can come for an hour".
- Preaching or prosecuting ("you deserve better", "he's using you").

Example: Leo asks for help moving on Saturday. The Owner is at their sister's and offers boxes and pizza Friday night. Instead of "Ahh I'd love to but I have a thing and my back's been weird, so sorry!!", send "Can't do Saturday, I'm at my sister's. Want me to bring boxes and pizza Friday night?"

Books: Grant, *Think Again*; Franco, *Platonic*; Fisher, *Getting to Yes*; Stone, *Thanks for the Feedback*; Scott, *Radical Candor*; Goldsmith, *What Got You Here Won't Get You There*; Kross, *Chatter*; Grenny, *Crucial Conversations*; Voss, *Never Split the Difference*.
